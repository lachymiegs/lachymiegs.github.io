Project Odyssey

ARES Rocketry's first ever student researched and developed hybrid rocket. Nitrous oxide and paraffin-ABS propellant, M-class motor targeting 10,000 ft. I lead the 120-member team developing it, managing everything from technical direction to sponsorship, policy, and competition strategy.

The program

Project Odyssey is ARES Rocketry's most ambitious undertaking — the team's first ever fully student-researched and developed hybrid rocket. Unlike previous ARES competition entries which flew commercial motors, Odyssey develops its own propulsion system from scratch: a nitrous oxide and paraffin-ABS hybrid motor targeting M-class thrust (~4.5 kN) to carry the vehicle to 10,000 ft.

The program spans six subteams — propulsion, structures, avionics, recovery, ground support, and systems — across 120 members. The target is AURC 2026, with IREC 2027 as the international competition goal where I will serve as flyer of record.

Leading the program

Taking on the Team Lead role meant stepping back from hands-on work and into a different kind of engineering problem — running a complex program with many moving parts, competing priorities, and real consequences for getting it wrong. The technical decisions still land with me, but the job is as much about removing blockers, maintaining momentum, and keeping 120 people aligned as it is about engineering.

My contributions to Odyssey include securing sponsorships that directly fund the program, writing high-level technical documentation for external stakeholders and competition officials, and introducing new ARES policy documentation to formalise how the organisation operates. On the operations side I have organised the hot fire campaign and key test events, managing the logistics, safety planning, and stakeholder communication those activities require.

External relationships are a significant part of the role — liaising with university faculty and the engineering department, managing sponsor relationships, coordinating with the Victorian Rocketry Association on launch operations, and engaging with AURC and IREC competition officials. Maintaining those relationships is what keeps the program moving.
